\chapter{Methodology}\label{chap:imp}


Pilot development
The experiment was refined through an initial pilot phase. The first pilot used 30 highly imageable words; however, the participant achieved perfect immediate recall, suggesting that the materials were insufficiently challenging. The word lists were subsequently drawn from a memorability-scored word dataset and initially balanced across easy, medium, and hard items. As recall performance remained high, list length was increased to 25 words. The final lists consisted of 25 low-memorability words, which produced a more suitable level of difficulty.
Early sessions also showed that the original questionnaire battery was overly long. The presence measure was therefore replaced with a shorter version to reduce participant burden.
Final experimental protocol
Before attending the session, participants were asked to draw a simple wall layout of their home. This layout was converted into a CSV file using a custom ChatGPT workflow and imported into the VR application.
At the beginning of the session, participants received an introduction to the method of loci. They then completed a short training task designed to familiarise them with the VR controls, the generic virtual environment, and the process of placing words at locations. During this task, participants memorised a short list of five words.
Participants next created their personalised virtual environment using their home layout. They could review and correct the imported wall layout if necessary, then furnished each room with approximately five items. This limit was intended to keep the customisation stage manageable while encouraging natural room-based grouping during recall. After saving their environment, participants took a short break and completed the first cognitive-load questionnaire.
The main task used a within-subjects design with two conditions: a generic environment (Condition A) and a personalised environment (Condition B). Two word lists were used, List 1 and List 2. Condition order and list assignment were counterbalanced: participants completed either the generic or personalised condition first, and each list was paired with each condition across participants. This produced four counterbalancing combinations: A.1/B.2 and B.1/A.2.
For each condition, participants had 10 minutes to place and memorise the assigned 25-word list in the relevant virtual environment. Recall was then delayed while participants completed questionnaires measuring cognitive load, enjoyment, perceived competence, and presence. Participants were subsequently given five minutes to verbally recall the word list. Following a short break, they completed the same procedure in the remaining condition with the other word list.
