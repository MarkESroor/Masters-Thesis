Title: Using Presence Questionnaires in Reality
Citation: Martin Usoh, Ernest Catena, Sima Arman, and Mel Slater, 2000, Presence: Teleoperators and Virtual Environments. DOI: 10.1162/105474600566989
Source: https://doi.org/10.1162/105474600566989
PDF in Papers: Yes — Papers\questionnaires\Using_Presence_Questionnaires_in_Reality.pdf.

Abstract (paraphrased):
The authors tested whether two presence questionnaires distinguish a real office experience from a VR simulation of the same office. Ten people completed a search task in each environment group.

Most important findings:
- The Slater-Usoh-Steed questionnaire scored only marginally higher in the real environment; the Witmer-Singer questionnaire showed no significant real-versus-virtual difference.
- The authors question whether these scales can fairly compare experiences across real and virtual environments, although they may still be useful when everyone uses the same type of environment.

Future work:
Further work should validate presence measures across environments and avoid treating a higher questionnaire score as a direct measure of learning or memory.
