Title: A Memory Palace for Brain Anatomy and Function Represented in Virtual Reality
Citation: Ludivine Blais, Hossain Samar Qorbani, Ali Arya, and Jim Davies, 2022, 19th International Conference on Cognition and Exploratory Learning in the Digital Age.
Source: https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED626951.pdf
PDF in Papers: Yes — A Memory Palace for Brain Anatomy and Function Represented in Virtual Reality.pdf.

Abstract (paraphrased):
The authors present a WebXR memory-palace experience designed to teach the names, functions, and locations of brain regions. The paper addresses a lack of VR memory-palace applications designed specifically for neuroscience education.

Most important findings:
- The contribution is primarily the learning-system design and implementation.
- It does not report a controlled learning-gain or delayed-retention result, so engagement or promise should not be treated as evidence of improved learning.

Future work:
The authors identify evaluation of actual learning gains and retention over time as the next step.
