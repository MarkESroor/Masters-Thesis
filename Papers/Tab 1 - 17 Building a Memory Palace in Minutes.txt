Title: Building a Memory Palace in Minutes: Equivalent Memory Performance Using Virtual Versus Conventional Environments with the Method of Loci
Citation: Eric L. G. Legge, Christopher R. Madan, Enoch T. Ng, and Jeremy B. Caplan, 2012, Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition.
Source: https://www.psych.ualberta.ca/~cml/papers/Legge_et_al_2012.pdf
PDF in Papers: Yes — Building a Memory Palace in Minutes - Virtual vs Conventional Environments.pdf.

Abstract (paraphrased):
The study asks whether a quickly created virtual environment can serve as a useful memory palace compared with a familiar or imagined conventional environment. Participants used the Method of Loci to learn word lists.

Most important findings:
- With participants who followed the instructed strategy, memory performance was broadly equivalent between virtual and conventional environments.
- The result suggests that a useful spatial structure can matter more than photorealistic appearance. Strategy compliance affected the analysis.

Future work:
The paper recommends measuring whether participants actually use the mnemonic, since non-compliance can hide or distort differences between conditions.
